# Understand how treatment is sequenced

**For most stage II–III triple-negative breast cancer, treatment runs in one order: about six months of chemotherapy with the immunotherapy drug pembrolizumab, then surgery, then radiation when indicated, then about six more months of pembrolizumab, with extra medicine if cancer remains at surgery.**

This guide explains that sequence and why it runs in that order. It covers nonmetastatic [triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC)](/wiki/education/concepts/breast-cancer/receptors-and-subtypes/tnbc), including locally advanced disease. Individual plans change with disease extent, side effects and genetics, so the lessons also show how to read the plan you are given.

## The standard plan at a glance

```mermaid
flowchart TD
  A["Weeks 1–12: paclitaxel, carboplatin, pembrolizumab (4 cycles)"] --> B["Weeks 13–24: doxorubicin or epirubicin, cyclophosphamide, pembrolizumab (4 cycles)"]
  B --> C["Surgery: pathology shows pCR or residual cancer"]
  C --> D["Radiation when indicated"]
  C --> E["Pembrolizumab, up to 9 more cycles"]
  C --> F{"Residual cancer?"}
  F -- "Yes" --> G["Ask about capecitabine or a trial"]
  F -- "Yes, and a germline BRCA1/2 variant" --> H["Ask about 1 year of olaparib"]
  style C fill:#fef3c7,stroke:#d97706
  style F fill:#dbeafe,stroke:#2563eb
```

*The KEYNOTE-522 schedule, with cycles every 3 weeks, and the main branches after surgery. Radiation and the extra medicines depend on findings; they are not given to everyone.*

This is the [KEYNOTE-522 regimen](/wiki/education/concepts/breast-cancer/treatment-setting/the-keynote-522-regimen). In the trial of 1,174 people, adding pembrolizumab raised [pathologic complete response (pCR)](/wiki/education/concepts/breast-cancer/surgery-and-pathology-results/pcr) from 51.2% to 64.8% and 5-year overall survival from 81.7% to 86.6%.

## The big ideas

1. **Systemic treatment comes first** because it treats the whole body early, lets surgery measure the response, and lets that response choose what follows.
2. **Surgery is also a test.** No invasive cancer in the breast and nodes (pCR) predicts a much better outlook; residual cancer opens extra options.
3. **What follows surgery depends on the findings.** Pembrolizumab continues either way. Residual cancer adds capecitabine as a tested option; residual cancer in someone with a germline *BRCA1* or *BRCA2* variant also adds olaparib, which OlympiA tested in that high-risk group.
4. **Breast, node and radiation choices have their own evidence.** Lumpectomy with radiation matches mastectomy for survival in eligible patients.
5. **A planned course and the delivered course are different records.** Holds and substitutions need their own explanations.

## What is still uncertain

- Whether adding capecitabine or olaparib to postoperative pembrolizumab helps; neither combination has a reported randomized trial.
- Whether antibody-drug conjugates beat standard options after residual disease; three phase 3 trials are under way.
- How much the postoperative pembrolizumab phase adds by itself, since KEYNOTE-522 randomized the whole strategy.
