# Start here: triple-negative breast cancer in 30 minutes

**Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is breast cancer that lacks the three targets most breast-cancer drugs aim at, so chemotherapy and immunotherapy carry treatment. For stage II–III disease, that treatment comes before surgery, and the surgery shows how well it worked.**

These three short lessons give a family member the core facts and one habit for reading the numbers that come with them.

## The big ideas

1. **Cancer is the body's own cells growing without normal controls**, able to invade nearby tissue and spread. A biopsy report describes what kind, how abnormal it looks (grade) and how far it has spread (stage); those are different labels.
2. **TNBC is negative for estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR) and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2).** It makes up about 15% of breast cancers, is more often diagnosed at a younger age, in Black women and in carriers of *BRCA1* or *BRCA2* variants, and grows faster than other invasive breast cancers [1]. Hormone and HER2 drugs do not apply.
3. **The immune system can recognize cancer, and cancers learn to switch it off.** T cells read small protein fragments on a cell's surface. T cells also carry a brake receptor, programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1), and many tumors display its partner, programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), to press it. Pembrolizumab blocks PD-1 and releases the brake.
4. **The standard plan for stage II–III TNBC** is about six months of chemotherapy with pembrolizumab, then surgery, then radiation when indicated and about six more months of pembrolizumab. If cancer remains at surgery, more treatment may be added [2].
5. **Read every number as "who, compared with what, by when."** Adding pembrolizumab raised the share with no invasive cancer at surgery from 51% to 65%, but raised 5-year survival from about 82% to about 87% [2]. Those are different questions with different answers.
