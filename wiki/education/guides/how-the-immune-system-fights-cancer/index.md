# Why the immune system doesn't just kill a tumor, and what checkpoint inhibitors change

**T cells can recognize cancer, but tumors survive by hiding the target, keeping T cells out or switching them down. Checkpoint inhibitors release one of those brakes; in early triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), adding one to chemotherapy improved survival.**

This guide follows an immune response from the first clue to its effect on a tumor, then places a checkpoint drug on that route. Plan on about 95 minutes across nine lessons.

## The big ideas

1. **A T cell sees a fragment, not a whole cell.** It recognizes a short protein piece displayed on [human leukocyte antigen (HLA)](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/antigen-presentation/hla). No display, no recognition.
2. **Starting a response and reaching the tumor are separate jobs.** Dendritic cells must prime T cells in a lymph node, and primed cells must then cross vessels and stroma to touch cancer cells.
3. **Tumors are shaped by the attack.** Immune pressure removes visible cancer cells; survivors hide targets, exclude T cells or exhaust them. This is [immunoediting](/wiki/education/concepts/cancer-biology/what-cancer-is/immunoediting).
4. **Checkpoint inhibitors lift one late brake.** They help T cells that already recognize and reach the tumor; they cannot supply a missing target.
5. **Markers do different jobs.** Stromal tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (sTILs) predict outcome after chemotherapy; programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) picks who gains from a checkpoint inhibitor only in advanced TNBC.

## Check your understanding

A tumor sample is full of T cells and stains PD-L1-positive. Why is that encouraging, and what is still open?

**One answer:** T cells reached the tumor, and PD-L1 there is often induced by T cells attacking it. Still open: whether those cells recognize the cancer, whether the tumor has lost HLA or [beta-2 microglobulin (B2M)](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/antigen-presentation/b2m) display, and whether a checkpoint drug adds benefit in this setting.

## What is still uncertain

- Whether checkpoint benefit in early TNBC comes from treatment before surgery, after it, or both; KEYNOTE-522 was not designed to separate them. The [perioperative immune window](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/tumor-immunology/perioperative-immune-window) explains why the timing is a biological question.
- No validated marker yet identifies which early-TNBC patients gain from pembrolizumab.
- How much of a response comes from reviving T cells already in the tumor versus recruiting new ones.
- Whether opening an exclusion barrier helps people; so far that has been shown mainly in mice.
