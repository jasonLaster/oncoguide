Panel 1: "1  Check the sample". A collected tissue piece enters a culture dish. A small magnifying cutaway distinguishes rounded violet cells labeled "Malignant cells" from differently shaped sage and blue cells labeled "Non-malignant cells". These are schematic identities, not identities established by shape alone. Put "Viable sample" and "Cell identity" beside the appropriate dish and cutaway. Do not draw patients or identifying information.

Panel 2: "2  Compare all four". Draw four matched culture wells in a clear 2-by-2 arrangement. Exact labels: "Vehicle", "Drug A", "Drug B", "A + B". Each well has the same illustrative starting cell mixture and cell count; no qualitative killing or benefit result is encoded. Drug A is one sage capsule-shaped symbol. Drug B is one blue hexagonal symbol. The A+B condition visibly has BOTH separate symbols, with a plus sign between them, never a fused capsule. Vehicle has neither active-drug symbol. Under the wells: "Replicates and dose ranges". Symbols are schematic treatment labels, not molecular structures or routes of administration.

Panel 3: "3  Different readouts". Draw three clearly separated, labeled assay views. "Viability": an assay instrument reading a dish, with a blank generic display and no efficacy-colored curve. "Cell death": a separate specimen dish with an intact cell and a visibly fragmented dead cell, emphasizing this is a different measurement; no arrow from viability to death. "Regrowth?": living intact cells labeled "Survivors" on the left, followed by a small washout/removal step labeled "Washout", and then an arrow to a later dish of intact living cells. This arrow begins only with survivors; it must never begin with dead cells or cell fragments. Regrowth is a question under the assay's culture conditions, not an assured outcome. No arrow connects the viability view or death view to the survivor/regrowth view. Remove the tiny flux/PARP mechanism inset; the surrounding lesson carries those separate mechanism measurements. No numerical assay result, dose, time or patient outcome is depicted.

Panel 4: "4  Keep claims separate". Three small physical evidence scenes with exact labels "Effect in this model", "Mechanism supported?", "Patient benefit proven?". Use a culture dish, a magnified generic molecular interaction, and an empty clinical-study chart with only unlabeled axes respectively. Dashed connectors with question marks separate the scenes; no success checkmark and no outcome curves. Under them: "Different evidence needed". They are distinct evidence questions, not a promise that completing one guarantees the next.

Accuracy constraints: reduced metabolic viability signal does not itself establish irreversible death. Death and regrowth are distinct readouts. Only surviving living cells can regrow after drug removal. This schematic mixed specimen includes non-malignant cells; measured cell identity is required, and their presence does not by itself constitute a separately validated healthy-cell control assay. All four exposure conditions are required for this comparison. No single assay automatically measures every illustrated endpoint. No patient benefit, recurrence-prevention claim or unqualified synergy result is depicted. All cell counts, assay icons and axes are illustrative, not measured data.

Negative constraints: no dead-cell-to-regrowth arrow; no merged A+B symbol; no extra cell populations or clinical curves; no moral red/green efficacy coding, labels beyond those listed, proprietary assay names, logos, watermarks, doctors, patients, photorealism or decorative arrows.
