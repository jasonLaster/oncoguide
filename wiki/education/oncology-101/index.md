# Oncology 101: immune foundations

**Start with four questions: what binds, how a response begins, what the cells can do and where the route can fail.**

Cancer and immunotherapy vocabulary becomes easier when each word has a job. These lessons help you read a mechanism diagram and ask a useful next question. Each includes a fictional example and a short practice exercise.

For an ordered path that adds tissue patterns, immune measurements and treatment comparisons, use [the immune-system guide](/wiki/education/guides/how-the-immune-system-fights-cancer/index). This page is the supporting lesson shelf for that path.

## Choose a lesson

| Lesson | The question you will answer | Suggested minutes |
|---|---|---|
| [Ask what happens after binding](/wiki/education/oncology-101/1-ligands) | Does the interaction signal, block or deliver something? | 8 |
| [How dendritic cells help start a response](/wiki/education/oncology-101/2-dendritic-cells) | How does a displayed clue become a priming opportunity? | 8 |
| [Why a T-cell count does not tell the whole story](/wiki/education/oncology-101/3-t-cell-states) | What separates presence, recognition and function? | 8 |
| [Find the step an immune response can lose](/wiki/education/oncology-101/4-escape-mechanisms) | Which dependency failed, and what would test a bypass? | 9 |

Reading times are suggested budgets. You can pause at a linked concept or use a lesson’s “Explain it back” prompt to check your understanding.
