# Questions for a targeted-therapy proposal

**A useful proposal names the product, the clinical setting, the evidence and the action being considered.** A target shortlist leaves too much unstated to assess benefit, safety or readiness.

**Before you start:** [Target versus therapeutic format](/wiki/education/concepts/therapeutics/antibody-based/target-vs-chassis) separates what is recognized from what acts. [Actionability](/wiki/education/concepts/evidence-and-statistics/biomarkers/actionability) separates a finding from a usable option. [On-target, off-tumor toxicity](/wiki/education/concepts/therapeutics/toxicities-and-supportive-care/on-target-off-tumor-toxicity) explains why accurate recognition can still harm healthy tissue.

## Where this step sits

After [mechanism](/wiki/education/targeted-therapy-modalities/01-mechanism) and [dependencies](/wiki/education/targeted-therapy-modalities/03-overlap-and-sequencing), turn the proposal into questions another person can answer. This worksheet supports the [surface-target guide](/wiki/education/guides/aiming-a-drug-at-a-surface-target/index). Next comes the [sample budget](/wiki/education/targeted-therapy-modalities/05-tissue-and-sample-budget).

## Make the question small enough to answer

Think of a proposal as a recipe. Naming an ingredient does not tell you which dish is planned, who it is for or whether it has been tested. The analogy stops there: treatment involves uncertain biological effects and risks, so a completed recipe is no proof of benefit.

Start with: “We are deciding whether to ____.” Reviewing existing data, consuming tissue for an experiment, making a product and giving treatment are different actions. Each needs its own rationale and responsible owner.

## Ask in this order

| Question | What a useful answer contains |
|---|---|
| What clinical problem is being addressed? | Disease setting, treatment intent, alternatives and measurable goal |
| What is the target? | Exact molecule or peptide–[human leukocyte antigen (HLA)](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/antigen-presentation/hla) complex, specimen and method |
| Is it accessible and sufficiently selective? | Protein location and normal-tissue evidence, with sampling limits |
| What is the proposed format? | Binder, payload or cell design and the complete product's mechanism |
| What evidence supports the proposed action? | Relevant human comparison, or named preclinical evidence and its limits |
| What is the safety plan? | Product-specific risks, monitoring, management capability and stopping rules |
| What must happen before use? | Manufacture, quality release, sponsor, site and applicable access pathway |
| What is being decided now? | Data review, research, manufacturing or administration, with a named owner |

For result-to-action branches, use the existing [decision ledger](/wiki/education/guides/judging-a-test-or-vendor-claim/03-connect-evidence-to-a-decision). It covers positive, negative and failed results, burden and stopping rules. This worksheet supplies the product-specific details.

## Keep the questions specific to the product

For an [antibody-drug conjugate (ADC)](/wiki/education/concepts/therapeutics/antibody-based/antibody-drug-conjugate), ask how its antibody, linker and payload work together. Which delivery steps and resistance mechanisms were tested? Internalization often matters, but its meaning depends on the design; avoid a universal yes/no rule. [Food and Drug Administration (FDA): ADC guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/155997/download).

For a [T-cell engager](/wiki/education/concepts/therapeutics/antibody-based/t-cell-engager) or cellular therapy, ask about [cytokine release syndrome (CRS)](/wiki/education/concepts/therapeutics/toxicities-and-supportive-care/cytokine-release-syndrome), [immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS)](/wiki/education/concepts/therapeutics/toxicities-and-supportive-care/icans), other neurologic effects, healthy-tissue recognition and cell access. What persists after dosing stops, and how is it managed? A living product needs a persistence plan. [FDA cell-product guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/156896/download).

For a [radioligand](/wiki/education/concepts/therapeutics/antibody-based/radioligand-therapy), ask how imaging relates to the therapeutic product's distribution and absorbed radiation dose. Which organ limits apply? Tracer uptake alone does not establish benefit or a safety threshold for [chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cells](/wiki/education/concepts/therapeutics/cell-therapies/car-anatomy). [FDA radiopharmaceutical guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/129547/download).

For [T-cell receptor (TCR)](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/t-cells/t-cell-receptor)-based therapy, ask which peptide and HLA allele are recognized. How were specificity, presentation and function tested? Receptor sequences or expanded clones alone leave those questions unanswered. [Primary TCR engineering study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16946036/).

## Worked example: fill a proposal card

Everything in this example is fictional. Product Q is an investigational ADC against surface protein M, with a specified antibody, cleavable linker and cell-damaging payload. A research team proposes reviewing Q for adults with a progressing metastatic solid cancer after prior treatment. The immediate question is whether existing data justify designing a laboratory experiment. No treatment, manufacturing or new sample collection is proposed.

The supplied dossier contains M [ribonucleic acid (RNA)](/wiki/education/concepts/biology-basics/molecules/rna-and-mrna) measurements from one tumor biopsy and Q activity in target-bearing cultured cells. It provides no human comparison, no direct tumor-surface measurement and no relevant healthy-tissue testing. Supplier participation, access and site arrangements are unknown.

| Proposal field | Completed answer |
|---|---|
| Product | Q, the specified ADC; evidence from another M-targeted format cannot fill its gaps |
| Setting and goal | Progressing metastatic cancer after prior treatment; review whether a laboratory question is worth pursuing |
| Measured finding | Tumor RNA signal and cultured-cell activity, under the supplied assay conditions |
| What it supports | A research hypothesis about M and Q; no demonstrated clinical benefit |
| Evidence gap | Accessible tumor protein, healthy-tissue effects, complete-product delivery and relevant human outcomes |
| Action now | Research lead reviews existing methods, controls and product data before proposing an experiment |
| Burden and stop | No tissue consumed by this review; pause experiment planning if product identity or usable evidence remains unavailable |
| Next question | “What Q-specific data address these gaps, and which result would change the experiment plan?” |

RNA abundance is not a direct measurement of accessible surface protein. Measuring protein would address another premise; it would still leave benefit unproven. Primary proteogenomic work measures RNA and protein as separate layers. [Mertins et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27251275/).

## Check access without turning it into evidence

An [investigational new drug (IND) application and a biologics license application (BLA)](/wiki/education/concepts/access-and-regulation/trials-and-access/ind-vs-bla) concern different milestones: defined investigational use and a request for marketing authorization. An effective IND is not marketing approval; BLA submission is not BLA approval. [FDA IND overview](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/types-applications/investigational-new-drug-application-ind), [FDA BLA overview](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/development-approval-process-cber/biologics-license-applications-blas-cber-regulated-products).

Use [the readiness lesson](/wiki/education/guides/accessing-experimental-therapy/03-build-the-readiness-picture) for supply, consent, review, delivery and monitoring. A manufacturing quote cannot confirm all those gates. Even confirmed access would answer a different question from whether treatment helps in the proposed setting.

## What can go wrong at this step

- **Expression becomes benefit.** A molecular result does not establish a supported treatment action.
- **One format borrows another's evidence.** Sharing a target does not establish the same delivery or safety.
- **An experiment becomes a commitment.** Name tissue, time and opportunity costs before expanding the action.
- **Availability becomes a promise.** Speculative development does not guarantee future access or protection against recurrence.

## Try it

A second fictional proposal says: “M RNA is abundant after complete tumor removal. We should manufacture Q now and give it to prevent recurrence. Its IND is active, and a supplier can make it.” You have only the dossier above.

Fill this card before reading the answer: **product ____; setting and goal ____; evidence ____; requested action ____; missing premise ____; next question ____.**

**Worked answer:** Q is the specified ADC. The setting is now postoperative recurrence prevention, unlike the metastatic setting discussed earlier. The evidence remains one biopsy's RNA signal and cultured-cell activity. The request adds manufacturing and treatment commitments. Neither those findings, an attributed IND milestone nor a supply offer establishes benefit in this new setting. Ask the clinical team what Q-specific human evidence or relevant protocol addresses recurrence prevention. Ask the sponsor which product and protocol the IND covers. The readiness owner must separately assess delivery requirements. With this dossier alone, the supported next step is evidence and pathway review; manufacture or administration remains unjustified by the supplied information.

## Explain it back

Complete: “The finding is ____. The product and setting are ____. Before deciding to ____, we need ____, and ____ owns the next answer.”

**One possible answer:** “An RNA signal plus cultured-cell activity. They concern Q in a proposed postoperative setting. Before committing to manufacture or treatment, we need relevant clinical evidence and product-specific safety. The clinical team owns the clinical assessment; the sponsor confirms the applicable product and protocol.”

## Takeaway

**Name the missing premise and its owner before a promising finding becomes a treatment assumption.**
