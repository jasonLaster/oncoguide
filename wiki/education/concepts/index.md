# Education concepts

Use a concept to answer one question; use a [guide](/wiki/education/index) to learn toward a goal. These summaries and the glossary definitions are generated from the concept pages. Domain and learner review remain pending where stated on each page.

## Access and regulation

| Concept | In one sentence |
|---|---|
| [Drug labeling and off-label use](/wiki/education/concepts/access-and-regulation/approval/drug-label-and-off-label-use) | Off-label use means a clinician uses an approved drug outside its approved labeling, while the evidence for that particular use must be assessed separately. |
| [Research-use, laboratory certification and test authorization](/wiki/education/concepts/access-and-regulation/approval/ruo-ldt-clia-and-fda-tiers) | Research-use labeling, laboratory certification and product authorization answer different questions about a test and do not form one universal ladder of clinical usefulness. |
| [Clinical trials versus expanded access](/wiki/education/concepts/access-and-regulation/trials-and-access/clinical-trial-vs-expanded-access) | A clinical trial studies a treatment under a research protocol, while expanded access uses an investigational product for treatment outside a trial under applicable safeguards. |

## Biology basics

| Concept | In one sentence |
|---|---|
| [Central dogma: from DNA to RNA to protein](/wiki/education/concepts/biology-basics/genetics/central-dogma) | For a protein-coding gene, cells transcribe DNA into RNA and translate the RNA into protein. |
| [Germline vs somatic: where a variant belongs](/wiki/education/concepts/biology-basics/genetics/germline-vs-somatic) | Germline variants can be passed through eggs or sperm, while somatic variants arise in other cell lineages. |
| [Mutation types: what changed in the DNA?](/wiki/education/concepts/biology-basics/genetics/mutation-types) | A DNA variant describes a difference in sequence or copy structure; its biological effect is a separate question. |
| [Ligands and receptors](/wiki/education/concepts/biology-basics/signaling/ligand-and-receptor) | A ligand is a molecule that binds a particular partner; a receptor is a protein that senses a signal and can change a cell’s behavior. |

## Breast cancer and pathology

| Concept | In one sentence |
|---|---|
| [Histologic grade](/wiki/education/concepts/breast-cancer/grade-and-stage/histologic-grade) | Histologic grade describes a breast cancer's microscopic features; the Nottingham system combines tubule formation, nuclear appearance and mitotic activity into grades 1, 2 or 3. |
| [Ki-67 proliferation index](/wiki/education/concepts/breast-cancer/grade-and-stage/ki-67) | Ki-67 is a protein associated with the active cell cycle; its proliferation index is the percentage of evaluated tumor-cell nuclei that stain positive under a specified method. |
| [TNM staging](/wiki/education/concepts/breast-cancer/grade-and-stage/tnm-staging) | TNM staging describes the primary tumor, regional lymph nodes and distant metastasis using a cancer-specific classification. |
| [Estrogen receptor (ER)](/wiki/education/concepts/breast-cancer/receptors-and-subtypes/estrogen-receptor) | The estrogen receptor (ER) is a protein inside cells that responds to estrogen; in breast cancer, ER status describes tumor-cell receptor staining under validated testing rules. |
| [HER2: receptor biology and test status](/wiki/education/concepts/breast-cancer/receptors-and-subtypes/her2) | Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) is a cell-surface signaling protein; clinical HER2 status describes overexpression or gene amplification under breast-cancer testing rules. |
| [Intrinsic breast-cancer subtype](/wiki/education/concepts/breast-cancer/receptors-and-subtypes/intrinsic-subtype) | An intrinsic breast-cancer subtype is a category assigned by comparing a tumor sample's gene-expression pattern with reference patterns. |
| [Progesterone receptor (PR)](/wiki/education/concepts/breast-cancer/receptors-and-subtypes/progesterone-receptor) | The progesterone receptor (PR) is a protein inside cells that responds to progesterone; breast-cancer PR status reports receptor staining in tumor-cell nuclei. |
| [Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC)](/wiki/education/concepts/breast-cancer/receptors-and-subtypes/tnbc) | Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is breast cancer classified as negative for estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) by validated tissue tests. |
| [Pathologic complete response (pCR)](/wiki/education/concepts/breast-cancer/surgery-and-pathology-results/pcr) | Pathologic complete response means no residual invasive cancer in the breast or sampled regional nodes after neoadjuvant treatment. |
| [Residual cancer burden (RCB)](/wiki/education/concepts/breast-cancer/surgery-and-pathology-results/residual-cancer-burden) | Residual cancer burden combines breast tumor-bed measurements and regional-node findings into a score after neoadjuvant treatment. |
| [Surgical margins](/wiki/education/concepts/breast-cancer/surgery-and-pathology-results/surgical-margins) | A surgical margin is the edge of removed tissue, examined to determine whether cancer reaches that edge. |
| [Neoadjuvant versus adjuvant therapy](/wiki/education/concepts/breast-cancer/treatment-setting/neoadjuvant-vs-adjuvant) | Neoadjuvant and adjuvant describe treatment's timing around the main treatment, usually surgery: neoadjuvant comes before it, and adjuvant comes after it to lower recurrence risk. |

## Cancer biology

| Concept | In one sentence |
|---|---|
| [Autophagy: recycling through lysosomes](/wiki/education/concepts/cancer-biology/cell-death-and-survival/autophagy) | Autophagy delivers cellular material to lysosomes for breakdown and reuse; measuring how much material is present does not establish how fast it moves. |
| [Ferroptosis: death through uncontrolled lipid oxidation](/wiki/education/concepts/cancer-biology/cell-death-and-survival/ferroptosis) | Ferroptosis is a form of cell death driven by iron-dependent damage to membrane lipids when protective systems cannot contain it. |
| [Ubiquitin and the proteasome](/wiki/education/concepts/cancer-biology/cell-death-and-survival/ubiquitin-and-proteasome) | The ubiquitin–proteasome system marks selected proteins for controlled breakdown, while other ubiquitin signals have jobs beyond destruction. |
| [Homologous recombination and HRD](/wiki/education/concepts/cancer-biology/genome-instability-and-repair/homologous-recombination-and-hrd) | Homologous recombination repairs damaged DNA using a matching template; homologous recombination deficiency means this repair process is impaired. |
| [Cholesterol homeostasis through SCAP–SREBP](/wiki/education/concepts/cancer-biology/metabolism-and-stress/cholesterol-and-srebp) | SCAP–SREBP regulation helps cells adjust lipid production and uptake in response to their internal sterol supply. |
| [Oxidative phosphorylation: using a proton gradient to make ATP](/wiki/education/concepts/cancer-biology/metabolism-and-stress/oxidative-phosphorylation) | Oxidative phosphorylation links electron transport to a proton gradient that powers ATP production in mitochondria. |
| [Notch signaling: a message between neighboring cells](/wiki/education/concepts/cancer-biology/signaling-pathways/notch-signaling) | Notch signaling commonly begins with contact between a ligand-bearing cell and a receptor-bearing neighbor, leading to release of a fragment that regulates gene expression. |
| [NRF2–KEAP1: regulating a stress-defense program](/wiki/education/concepts/cancer-biology/signaling-pathways/nrf2-keap1) | KEAP1 helps control NRF2 turnover, while stabilized NRF2 can activate genes that protect cells from oxidative and chemical stress. |
| [PI3K–AKT–mTOR: growth signals and resource use](/wiki/education/concepts/cancer-biology/signaling-pathways/pi3k-akt-mtor) | PI3K–AKT–mTOR signaling links growth cues and available resources to cellular programs such as protein production, metabolism, and recycling. |
| [Wnt–β-catenin: signaling through controlled protein stability](/wiki/education/concepts/cancer-biology/signaling-pathways/wnt-beta-catenin) | Canonical Wnt signaling changes the control of β-catenin breakdown, allowing β-catenin to help regulate gene expression. |
| [Oncogenes and tumor suppressors: two routes to lost control](/wiki/education/concepts/cancer-biology/what-cancer-is/oncogene-and-tumor-suppressor) | Oncogenes promote cancer when growth-related functions become overactive, while tumor suppressors normally restrain cancer-promoting processes. |
| [Tumor heterogeneity: one cancer, several populations](/wiki/education/concepts/cancer-biology/what-cancer-is/tumor-heterogeneity) | Tumor heterogeneity means cancer cells can differ in their DNA, molecular state or behavior within and between sampled regions. |

## Evidence and statistics

| Concept | In one sentence |
|---|---|
| [Clinical actionability](/wiki/education/concepts/evidence-and-statistics/biomarkers/actionability) | Clinical actionability asks whether a finding supports a specific medical decision in the relevant disease and treatment setting. |
| [Prognostic versus predictive biomarkers](/wiki/education/concepts/evidence-and-statistics/biomarkers/prognostic-vs-predictive) | A prognostic biomarker is associated with outcome, while a predictive biomarker identifies differences in the effect of a particular treatment. |
| [Absolute risk, relative risk, and number needed to treat](/wiki/education/concepts/evidence-and-statistics/effect-measures/absolute-relative-risk-and-nnt) | Absolute risk is the chance of a defined event by a specified time; relative risk compares those chances, and number needed to treat summarizes their difference. |
| [Confidence interval](/wiki/education/concepts/evidence-and-statistics/effect-measures/confidence-interval) | A confidence interval shows an estimate's precision using a method with a stated rate of coverage across repeated studies. |
| [Hazard ratio](/wiki/education/concepts/evidence-and-statistics/effect-measures/hazard-ratio) | A hazard ratio compares the instantaneous event rates of two groups among people who remain at risk in each group. |
| [Kaplan–Meier curves](/wiki/education/concepts/evidence-and-statistics/endpoints/kaplan-meier) | A Kaplan–Meier curve estimates the probability of remaining free of a defined event over time while accounting for incomplete follow-up. |
| [Survival endpoints](/wiki/education/concepts/evidence-and-statistics/endpoints/survival-endpoints) | A survival endpoint specifies the starting time and which events count during follow-up. |
| [Lead-time bias](/wiki/education/concepts/evidence-and-statistics/reasoning-tools/lead-time-bias) | Lead-time bias occurs when earlier detection lengthens measured survival from diagnosis without changing when the outcome occurs. |
| [Controlled perturbation: change one thing and test the response](/wiki/education/concepts/evidence-and-statistics/study-design/perturbation-and-controls) | A controlled perturbation changes a defined experimental factor and compares the response with conditions that help explain what caused it. |
| [Trial phases and randomization](/wiki/education/concepts/evidence-and-statistics/study-design/trial-phases-and-randomization) | Trial phases describe the questions a treatment study asks, while randomization assigns participants by chance to make treatment groups more comparable. |
| [Positive predictive value and base rates](/wiki/education/concepts/evidence-and-statistics/tests-and-prediction/ppv-and-base-rates) | Positive predictive value is the probability of the defined condition among people with a positive test result. |

## Immunology

| Concept | In one sentence |
|---|---|
| [Antibody structure](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/antibodies-and-b-cells/antibody-structure) | An antibody is an immune protein whose binding regions recognize a molecular feature and whose remaining structure helps determine its biological functions. |
| [Antigen-processing machinery](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/antigen-presentation/antigen-processing-machinery) | Antigen-processing machinery generates protein fragments and loads them onto display molecules for T-cell inspection. |
| [B2M (beta-2 microglobulin)](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/antigen-presentation/b2m) | Beta-2 microglobulin supports the assembly and stable surface expression of classical human leukocyte antigen class-I complexes. |
| [Cross-presentation](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/antigen-presentation/cross-presentation) | Cross-presentation occurs when a cell displays externally acquired antigen on class-I human leukocyte antigen molecules. |
| [HLA (human leukocyte antigen)](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/antigen-presentation/hla) | HLA molecules display protein fragments for T-cell recognition. |
| [Neoantigen](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/antigen-presentation/neoantigen) | A neoantigen is an immune-recognition target arising from a tumor-specific sequence alteration. |
| [Cytokines and chemokines](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/immune-regulation/cytokine-and-chemokine) | Cytokines are signaling proteins that change cell behavior; chemokines are a cytokine family best known for helping direct cell movement. |
| [Immune checkpoints](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/immune-regulation/immune-checkpoint) | Immune checkpoints are regulatory interactions that adjust immune-cell responses, including inhibitory pathways that help protect healthy tissue. |
| [Natural killer cells and missing-self recognition](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/innate-and-unconventional-cells/nk-cell-and-missing-self) | Natural killer cells integrate activating and inhibitory signals, and reduced normal self-display can remove part of the inhibition that restrains them. |
| [T-cell priming and the three-signal model](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/t-cells/t-cell-priming-three-signals) | T-cell priming is the initial activation of a naive T cell by antigen recognition and a supporting signal context. |
| [T-cell states](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/t-cells/t-cell-states) | T-cell states describe changing differentiation and functional programs, rather than simply whether a T cell is present. |
| [TCR (T-cell receptor)](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/t-cells/t-cell-receptor) | A conventional alpha-beta T-cell receptor recognizes a peptide together with the human leukocyte antigen molecule displaying it. |
| [The T-cell repertoire](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/t-cells/t-cell-repertoire) | The T-cell repertoire is the collection of different T-cell receptors in a population and their abundances at a particular time. |
| [Innate vs adaptive immunity](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/the-immune-system/innate-vs-adaptive-immunity) | Innate immunity uses inherited sensing systems, while adaptive immunity expands cells with diverse antigen-specific receptors. |
| [The immune-cell roster](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/the-immune-system/immune-cell-roster) | Immune cells perform different sensing, presentation, killing, antibody-producing and regulatory jobs that depend on their state and location. |
| [Hot, excluded and cold tumor patterns](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/tumor-immunology/hot-excluded-cold-tumor) | Hot, excluded and cold describe patterns of immune-cell presence and location in sampled tumor tissue, rather than proving tumor recognition or treatment benefit. |
| [Immune escape](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/tumor-immunology/immune-escape) | Immune escape occurs when cancer cells survive by avoiding recognition, access or effective immune attack. |
| [The cancer-immunity cycle](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/tumor-immunology/cancer-immunity-cycle) | The cancer-immunity cycle is a framework connecting antigen release, T-cell priming, tumor access, recognition and killing in a repeating immune response. |

## Measurement and assays

| Concept | In one sentence |
|---|---|
| [Analytical validity, clinical validity and clinical utility](/wiki/education/concepts/measurement/computational-analysis/analytical-validity-clinical-validity-and-utility) | Analytical validity asks whether a test measures correctly, clinical validity asks whether its result relates to a clinical condition, and clinical utility asks whether using it helps patients. |
| [Virtual cell model: a computed biological response](/wiki/education/concepts/measurement/computational-analysis/virtual-cell-model) | A virtual cell model uses computation to represent selected cell properties or responses rather than growing a living experimental preparation. |
| [Ex vivo drug screen: response outside the original body](/wiki/education/concepts/measurement/functional-models/ex-vivo-drug-screen) | An ex vivo drug screen measures how collected living material responds to drug exposure outside its original organism. |
| [Organoid: a small culture with selected tissue features](/wiki/education/concepts/measurement/functional-models/organoid) | An organoid is a three-dimensional cell culture that self-organizes to reproduce selected structural or functional features of a tissue. |
| [Patient-derived xenograft: a tumor growing in another host](/wiki/education/concepts/measurement/functional-models/patient-derived-xenograft) | A patient-derived xenograft is a model made by implanting patient tumor material into an animal of another species. |
| [ELISpot (enzyme-linked immunospot)](/wiki/education/concepts/measurement/immune-monitoring/elispot) | ELISpot counts spots formed when sampled cells release a chosen molecule under defined test conditions. |
| [Peptide–HLA multimer staining](/wiki/education/concepts/measurement/immune-monitoring/peptide-hla-multimer-staining) | Multimer staining labels cells whose receptors bind a specified peptide held by its display molecule under the assay conditions. |
| [T-cell receptor sequencing](/wiki/education/concepts/measurement/immune-monitoring/tcr-sequencing) | T-cell receptor sequencing identifies sampled receptor sequences and their measured abundances, allowing immune populations to be compared across samples. |
| [Cell-free DNA versus circulating tumor DNA](/wiki/education/concepts/measurement/liquid-biopsy/cfdna-vs-ctdna) | Cell-free DNA is DNA outside intact cells in a sample; circulating tumor DNA is the portion that comes from tumor cells. |
| [ctDNA (circulating tumor DNA) units and detection limits](/wiki/education/concepts/measurement/liquid-biopsy/ctdna-units) | A circulating tumor DNA number is meaningful only with the assay’s measured quantity, units and detection criteria. |
| [Tumor-informed molecular residual disease assays](/wiki/education/concepts/measurement/liquid-biopsy/tumor-informed-mrd-assay) | A tumor-informed molecular residual disease assay uses features identified in a tumor sample to look for a qualifying tumor signal in later blood samples. |
| [Immunohistochemistry: protein staining in tissue](/wiki/education/concepts/measurement/pathology-readouts/immunohistochemistry) | Immunohistochemistry uses antibodies to visualize selected proteins in tissue while preserving information about the stained cells and their location. |
| [Stromal TIL scoring: what the percentage counts](/wiki/education/concepts/measurement/pathology-readouts/stil-scoring) | A stromal tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte score estimates the percentage of eligible tumor-associated stromal area occupied by mononuclear inflammatory cells. |
| [Immunopeptidomics](/wiki/education/concepts/measurement/proteins-and-spatial/immunopeptidomics) | Immunopeptidomics identifies protein fragments associated with antigen-display molecules in a sampled preparation. |
| [Panel, exome and genome sequencing: choose the coverage](/wiki/education/concepts/measurement/sequencing-basics/panel-exome-genome) | Panel, exome and genome sequencing examine progressively broader sets of DNA regions, with assay-specific depth and detection limits. |
| [Variant allele fraction vs cancer cell fraction](/wiki/education/concepts/measurement/sequencing-basics/variant-allele-fraction-and-cancer-cell-fraction) | Variant allele fraction measures variant-supporting sequence observations, while cancer cell fraction estimates how many cancer cells carry the change. |
| [Bulk RNA sequencing: a tissue mixture of messages](/wiki/education/concepts/measurement/transcriptomics/bulk-rna-seq) | Bulk RNA sequencing measures captured RNA from a sample as one combined profile, without directly assigning each message to its cell of origin. |

## Treatment mechanisms

| Concept | In one sentence |
|---|---|
| [ADC (antibody-drug conjugate)](/wiki/education/concepts/therapeutics/antibody-based/antibody-drug-conjugate) | An antibody-drug conjugate links a targeting antibody to a drug payload so binding and product-specific processing can deliver chemical damage. |
| [T-cell engager](/wiki/education/concepts/therapeutics/antibody-based/t-cell-engager) | A T-cell engager is a therapeutic molecule that binds a target on another cell and an activation component such as CD3 on T cells to help redirect T-cell attack. |
| [CAR (chimeric antigen receptor) anatomy](/wiki/education/concepts/therapeutics/cell-therapies/car-anatomy) | A chimeric antigen receptor connects an external antigen-binding region to internal signaling machinery in an engineered immune cell. |
| [Chemotherapy: disrupting cell survival and division](/wiki/education/concepts/therapeutics/chemotherapy/chemotherapy) | Cytotoxic chemotherapy uses drugs to disrupt processes such as copying DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) or dividing cells, affecting cancer cells and susceptible normal cells. |
| [Synthetic lethality](/wiki/education/concepts/therapeutics/how-drugs-work/synthetic-lethality) | Synthetic lethality occurs when two impairments together kill a cell even though either impairment alone can be tolerated. |
| [Immune checkpoint inhibitor](/wiki/education/concepts/therapeutics/immunotherapy/immune-checkpoint-inhibitor) | An immune checkpoint inhibitor is a drug that blocks a specific inhibitory immune pathway, which can strengthen responses against cancer and also harm healthy tissue. |
| [Cytokine release syndrome (CRS)](/wiki/education/concepts/therapeutics/toxicities-and-supportive-care/cytokine-release-syndrome) | Cytokine release syndrome is a systemic inflammatory response that can follow immune activation and cause fever, low blood pressure or low oxygen. |
| [Immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS)](/wiki/education/concepts/therapeutics/toxicities-and-supportive-care/icans) | ICANS is a neurologic syndrome associated with immune-effector-cell therapy that is assessed through clinical changes in brain function. |
| [Immune-related adverse event (irAE)](/wiki/education/concepts/therapeutics/toxicities-and-supportive-care/immune-related-adverse-event) | An immune-related adverse event is harm to healthy tissue attributed to treatment-related immune activity. |
| [On-target, off-tumor toxicity](/wiki/education/concepts/therapeutics/toxicities-and-supportive-care/on-target-off-tumor-toxicity) | On-target, off-tumor toxicity occurs when a treatment recognizes its intended target on healthy tissue and harms that tissue. |
