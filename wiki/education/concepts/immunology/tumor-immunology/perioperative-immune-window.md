# Perioperative immune window

## In one sentence

The perioperative immune window is the period of days to weeks around cancer surgery when surgical stress, anesthesia, pain control and wound healing temporarily change immune function.

## The intuition

Surgery is like major repair work on a house: necessary, and usually the step that removes the problem. While the work goes on, the building's security staff are busy with the repair, and their usual patrols thin out. Researchers ask whether any stray cancer cells use that short gap. The analogy has limits: the "gap" is measured mostly in blood tests and animals, and no change to the repair plan has yet been shown to make the house safer afterward.

## How it works

Several things happen at once around an operation ([Horowitz et al., 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25601442/)):

1. **The stress response.** Tissue injury and stress raise adrenaline-like hormones (catecholamines), prostaglandins and cortisol. A short inflammatory phase, with interleukin 6 (IL-6) rising, is followed by a longer anti-inflammatory phase.
2. **Immune cells shift.** [Natural killer (NK) cells](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/innate-and-unconventional-cells/nk-cell-and-missing-self) are among the most affected; their killing activity in blood falls after surgery. Suppressive myeloid cells and [regulatory T cells](/wiki/education/concepts/immunology/t-cells/regulatory-t-cells) can rise ([Angka et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28817109/)).
3. **Wound healing sends growth signals.** Repair brings new blood vessels, growth factors and transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β), signals that can also help cancer cells.
4. **Anesthesia and pain control add their own effects.** In laboratory studies, inhaled (volatile) anesthetics and opioids impair some immune functions. Regional nerve blocks and intravenous propofol reduce the stress response.

Most of this evidence comes from blood measurements in patients and from mouse models. Whether these changes alter recurrence in people is a separate question, and randomized trials answer it best.

## What the evidence shows, by level

| Claim | Evidence type | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Surgery temporarily lowers NK-cell activity and raises suppressive signals | Human blood studies; mouse models | A real, short-lived immune shift; its effect on recurrence is not measured by these studies |
| The anesthetic used during breast cancer surgery changes recurrence | **Randomized trials** | **No.** In 2,108 women, regional anesthesia with propofol versus general anesthesia with sevoflurane and opioids gave the same recurrence rate (10% vs 10%; hazard ratio 0.97) ([Sessler et al., 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31645288/)). In 1,670 women, propofol versus sevoflurane gave 5-year survival of 91.9% vs 92.2% (hazard ratio 1.03, 95% CI 0.73–1.44) ([Enlund et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37333664/)) |
| Blocking stress-hormone and prostaglandin signaling around surgery helps | Small randomized biomarker trial | In 38 women with early breast cancer, 11 days of propranolol (a beta-blocker) plus etodolac (a COX-2 inhibitor) improved tumor and blood markers linked to metastasis. Recurrence was not an endpoint ([Shaashua et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28490464/)) |
| Immunotherapy works better before tumor removal than after | Mouse models | In two metastatic mouse breast cancer models, immunotherapy given before removing the primary tumor beat the same treatment given after ([Liu et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27663893/)) |

## Why it matters in cancer

- **It explains a research question, not a reason to delay surgery.** Removing the tumor is the curative step. The window is about whether the days around it can be made safer.
- **Anesthetic choice for breast cancer surgery can be made on usual grounds.** Two randomized trials found no recurrence or survival difference.
- **It frames immunotherapy timing.** In KEYNOTE-522, pembrolizumab is given before and after surgery. Mouse data favor treating while the tumor is still present, but the trial was not designed to separate the two phases; see [the KEYNOTE-522 regimen](/wiki/education/concepts/breast-cancer/treatment-setting/the-keynote-522-regimen).

## Common confusions

- **"Surgery spreads cancer."** That is not what the evidence shows. Surgery cures many early cancers. The window concerns short-term immune changes, not a reason to avoid operating.
- **A blood measurement is not a tumor measurement.** Lower NK activity in blood after surgery does not show what immune cells do at a residual-disease site.
- **Observational differences are not trial results.** Retrospective studies suggested intravenous anesthesia might be better; the breast cancer randomized trials above did not confirm it.
- **A biomarker change is not fewer recurrences.** The propranolol plus etodolac trial measured markers, not outcomes.

## Try it

A friend reads that inhaled anesthetics suppress immune cells and asks for intravenous-only anesthesia for breast cancer surgery to lower her recurrence risk. What does the evidence say?

**Answer:** Laboratory and observational data raised the idea. Two randomized trials in breast cancer surgery, with more than 1,600 women each, found no difference in recurrence or survival. Anesthesia can be chosen on usual safety and comfort grounds.

## Explain it back

"Around surgery, ___ changes for days to weeks; randomized trials have shown that ___ does not change breast cancer recurrence."

**One possible answer:** "immune function, especially NK-cell activity; the choice between regional or intravenous and inhaled anesthesia."

## Takeaway

**Surgery shifts immune function briefly; that shift is real, but no change in anesthesia has yet been shown to lower breast cancer recurrence.**
