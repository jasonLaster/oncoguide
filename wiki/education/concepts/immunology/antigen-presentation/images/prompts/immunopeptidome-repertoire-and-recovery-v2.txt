Use case: scientific-educational
Asset type: portrait educational cartoon for the immunopeptidome concept's How it works section.
Primary request: Teach the physical difference between contextual peptide–HLA display and the method-dependent peptide list recovered by a laboratory. Make release and separation unmistakable before instrument input.

Composition: One portrait image, about 1024 by 1536, with four vertically stacked physical scenes. A large short heading tops each scene. Use generous whitespace, a consistent purple folded HLA shape, orange connected bead-chain peptides, and blue capture antibodies. Objects have meaningful depth, soft hand-painted texture, friendly rounded contours and crisp outlines. No people or generic report-card layout. Short top-to-bottom arrows indicate laboratory preparation steps, not immune activation.

Scene 1 — exact heading "Biological display": Two anonymous cutaway cells have purple peptide–HLA complexes at the membrane and in small intracellular vesicles. Each orange peptide is a continuous connected chain nestled in the HLA groove, never a cloud of disconnected dots. Short leader labels "HLA" and "Peptide" point to the correct parts of one magnified surface complex. Show a membrane, nucleus and vesicles without extra labels. Intracellular complexes are inside vesicles, not attached to the nucleus. Different peptide shapes are illustrative, not counted cell copies. No T cells.

Scene 2 — exact heading "Recover complexes": A prepared lysate tube contains varied sample material. A small arrow "Capture" leads to a magnified antibody-coated bead carrying purple HLA complexes with their connected orange peptide cargo still seated in the grooves. A leader label "Capture bead" points to the bead. This is laboratory recovery after preparation, not an antibody attacking a living cell. Show only a few schematic molecules; do not encode recovery fractions.

Scene 3 — exact heading "Release + separate": Show an intact recovered peptide–HLA complex on the left, then a clear process arrow to a tube labeled "Peptide input" on the right. The tube contains only distinct connected orange peptide chains with visible joins between amino-acid beads. Show the now-separated purple HLA protein beside a small side collection vessel, clearly outside the peptide tube and outside the continuing instrument route. The heading names both dissociation and peptide separation; do not imply acid release alone removes every other protein. Preserve the orange chain identity through recovery and release. Do not cut the peptide into free amino acids. No protein-digestion scissors.

Scene 4 — exact heading "Identify peptides": The same peptide-only tube feeds a compact chromatography-and-mass-spectrometry apparatus. A short connector labeled "Analyze" begins at the peptide tube, never at an intact HLA complex. A small monitor shows a few qualitative spectrum peaks and a small notebook shows several schematic sequence marks; neither is a complete census. There are no purple HLA complexes, beads, antibodies or whole cells at the instrument input. One short bottom line reads "A recovered list, not a census".

Typography: One clean sans-serif family. Very large sentence-case scene headings, large object labels, high contrast, no tiny footnotes. The headings and core object labels must remain easy to read when the entire portrait is scaled to a roughly 312-pixel mobile column. Use only the exact text named above; no numerical axes, percentages, molecule counts, sequence IDs or additional prose.

Accuracy constraints: This is a schematic class-I immunoaffinity-capture example within a broader HLA-associated repertoire concept, not a universal protocol. Whole-cell lysate can sample surface and intracellular complexes. The peptide is physically connected while bound and after release. Recovery, dissociation/separation and identification are distinct. The method-dependent identified list does not establish cell of origin in mixed tissue, a complete surface census, biological absence of undetected peptides, T-cell recognition, killing, safety or clinical benefit. Do not draw a cell-to-list one-to-one tally or a triumphant success/checkmark scene. Molecule shapes and quantities are schematic.

Avoid: Neon/glossy rendering, photorealism, medical photography, logos, watermarks, smiling cells, therapeutic effect, red/green good/bad coding, free peptide being shown as natural surface display, intact HLA complex as mass-spectrometry input, and disconnected orange dots used to represent released peptides.
