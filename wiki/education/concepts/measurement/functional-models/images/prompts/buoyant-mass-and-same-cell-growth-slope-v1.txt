Learner task: Distinguish buoyant mass from a repeated same-cell growth slope
Gap: No physical-measurement inset or mass-versus-time plot
Accuracy guardrails (verbatim): Distinguish buoyant mass from a same-cell mass-versus-time slope and state the assay units. A fractional growth-rate reduction is not equivalent to shrinkage, death or patient response.
Creative plan: A fluid-filled channel loops inside a suspended vibrating beam; a passing cell shifts its frequency, calibrated to buoyant mass. An invented graph follows the same toy cell at 0, 0.5 and 1 hour with masses of 10, 11 and 12 picograms; slope is 2 picograms per hour. A separate invented matched-group rate comparison of 2 versus 0.5 picograms per hour yields a 75 percent lower positive rate. Neither reduced rate nor buoyant mass alone demonstrates shrinkage, death or patient response.
Takeaway: Follow the same cell through repeated calibrated readings to estimate a slope, in picograms per hour. The toy values are invented: a fall from 2 to 0.5 pg/h is a 75% lower positive rate, not a killed-cell fraction, shrinkage or patient response.
Visual style: three linked filled inset teaching cards; transparent outer canvas; restrained hand-drawn cell, compartment and assay glyphs; personable teaching questions; large labels.
