# Biopsy after neoadjuvant therapy to predict pCR

## In one sentence

A biopsy after neoadjuvant therapy samples the former tumor site with image-guided needles to predict whether surgery would find no cancer; in published studies it missed between 3% and 50% of the cancers that surgery later found.

## The intuition

Imagine checking whether a garden bed is free of weeds by pulling a few plugs of soil instead of digging up the whole bed. If the plugs are clean, the bed is probably clear, but a few weeds between the plugs would be missed. Surgery digs up the bed; the biopsy pulls plugs.

The analogy understates one problem. After treatment, leftover cancer is often a few scattered cells rather than one clump, which makes it easier to miss between samples.

## How it works

Neoadjuvant (before-surgery) treatment often shrinks a breast tumor until imaging shows little or nothing. A [pathologic complete response (pCR)](/wiki/education/concepts/breast-cancer/surgery-and-pathology-results/pcr), meaning no invasive cancer left, can normally be confirmed only on the surgical specimen. The question is whether a smaller procedure could confirm it instead.

1. A marker clip placed at diagnosis shows where the tumor was.
2. Under imaging guidance, a [vacuum-assisted](/wiki/education/concepts/measurement/specimens/vacuum-assisted-biopsy) or [core needle](/wiki/education/concepts/measurement/specimens/core-needle-biopsy) device takes several samples from the tumor bed.
3. A pathologist looks for invasive cancer and in situ cancer in the samples.
4. In accuracy studies, surgery follows anyway, so every biopsy can be checked against the whole specimen.

Two numbers describe accuracy. The **false-negative rate** is, among people whose surgery found cancer, the share whose biopsy showed none. The **negative predictive value** is, among people with a negative biopsy, the share who truly had no cancer left. The second depends on how common pCR is in the group ([predictive values and base rates](/wiki/education/concepts/evidence-and-statistics/tests-and-prediction/ppv-and-base-rates)).

## What the trials found

| Study | Who and how | Result |
|---|---|---|
| RESPONDER ([Heil 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32657944/)) | 398 women, all subtypes, with an imaging response; vacuum-assisted biopsy, then surgery | Biopsy missed cancer in 37 of 208 with residual disease (false-negative rate 17.8%). Of the 37 misses, 20 had residual tumor under 5 mm |
| Pooled three-center data ([Tasoulis 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33026457/)) | 166 women; most had vacuum-assisted biopsy; surgery as reference | False-negative rate 18.7% when the biopsy was representative (159 women). In a subgroup with a clinical response, a residual lesion of 2 cm or less and at least 6 vacuum samples (76 women): 3.2%, negative predictive value 97.4% |
| NRG-BR005 ([Basik 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40332918/)) | 101 evaluable women with a complete clinical response and a complete or near-complete imaging response; multiple stereotactic cores; surgery as reference | 36 had residual invasive or in situ cancer. Biopsy found only half of them (sensitivity 50%); negative predictive value 78.3% |
| Surgery omission ([Kuerer 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40152443/)) | 50 women aged 40 or older with triple-negative or human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive cancer and a residual lesion under 2 cm; at least 12 vacuum cores | 31 (62%) had no cancer on biopsy and received radiation without breast surgery. No in-breast recurrences at a median 55.4 months |

Technique and selection change the result. Larger needles, more samples, smaller residual lesions and stricter imaging criteria all lowered the miss rate. In RESPONDER, an exploratory analysis found no false negatives with the largest (7-gauge) needle, and combining imaging with biopsy brought the false-negative rate to 6.2%.

## Why surgery is still standard

- **The miss rate is too high outside tight selection.** NRG-BR005 concluded that its results do not support omitting surgery. RESPONDER missed about 1 in 6 residual cancers.
- **Surgery provides information the biopsy cannot.** The whole specimen gives [residual cancer burden (RCB)](/wiki/education/concepts/breast-cancer/surgery-and-pathology-results/residual-cancer-burden) and margins. Residual disease is what triggers [post-neoadjuvant escalation](/wiki/education/concepts/breast-cancer/treatment-setting/post-neoadjuvant-escalation), such as capecitabine or olaparib.
- **The lymph nodes need their own assessment.** A breast biopsy says nothing about the axilla (see [sentinel node and axillary dissection](/wiki/education/concepts/breast-cancer/surgery-and-pathology-results/sentinel-node-and-axillary-dissection)).
- **Omission evidence is single-arm and small.** The omission trial had 31 women without surgery and no comparison group. Its authors called for more confirmatory studies before practice changes.

## Why it matters in cancer

Triple-negative and HER2-positive cancers reach pCR most often after neoadjuvant therapy; in the pooled data, 39 of 59 triple-negative tumors (66%) had pCR ([Tasoulis 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33026457/)). If surgery could be skipped safely for those patients, many would avoid an operation. That is why response-guided surgery is being studied. It also explains the caution: a missed residual cancer could mean missing both local control and the escalation that residual disease would have triggered.

## Common confusions

- **"A negative biopsy proves pCR."** It shows no cancer in the samples taken.
- **"An imaging complete response is a pCR."** NRG-BR005 enrolled only people with a complete clinical response and a complete or near-complete imaging response, and 36 of 101 still had cancer at surgery.
- **"False-negative rate and negative predictive value are the same."** They divide by different groups; a test can have a high negative predictive value simply because pCR is common.
- **"No recurrences in a small trial proves safety."** Zero events in 31 people still leaves real uncertainty.

## Try it

A fictional trial biopsies 100 people, then operates. Surgery finds cancer in 40. The biopsy was negative in 8 of those 40 and negative in all 60 who had pCR. What are the false-negative rate and the negative predictive value?

**Answer:** False-negative rate = 8 / 40 = 20%. Negative biopsies = 60 + 8 = 68, so negative predictive value = 60 / 68, about 88%. The same biopsy sounds better or worse depending on which number is quoted.

## Explain it back

"A clean biopsy after treatment can miss cancer because ___, and surgery remains standard because ___."

**One possible answer:** "the needles sample only part of a tumor bed where leftover cells are often scattered; it is more accurate and also gives residual burden, margins and node status that guide further treatment."

## Takeaway

**A post-treatment biopsy can suggest pCR, but outside trials the surgical specimen still decides it.**
