Scene: A radiolabeled Y-shaped binder travels from blood to tissue, where it competes with an unlabeled binder for targets. Text notes that clearance and retained label alter signal over time. Signal includes blood delivery, access, binding and retention at a specified imaging time.
Caption: An uptake image records the tested tracer’s distribution at a particular time.
Explain: The orange dot marks the radioactive label; the blue Y is the binder. A gray, unlabeled binder can occupy the same target without adding PET signal. Delivery from blood, access to tissue, competition, clearance and retention all shape the recorded distribution. The diagram is conceptual and does not assign uptake values. Record the isotope and binder format together with protein mass, activity and time. For example, the trastuzumab feasibility study tested a full antibody labeled with zirconium-89 over a multi-day imaging window; the gallium-68 nanobody study used a different binder and earlier scans. Their timing is study-specific, not an interchangeable imaging rule.
Science guards: Match isotope, binder format and imaging time to the claim. Delivery, competition/blocking, clearance and retention affect uptake; tracer signal is not target abundance alone, therapeutic dose or benefit.
