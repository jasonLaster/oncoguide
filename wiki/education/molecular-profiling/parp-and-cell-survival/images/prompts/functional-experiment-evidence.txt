Teach how to evaluate a functional drug-combination test from specimen quality through controls, time, and evidence limits.
Title "From a living sample to an interpretable result".
Panel 1 heading "1  Check the sample". Draw tissue entering a lab dish and magnified heterogeneous cells, with malignant cells explicitly distinct from others. Labels "Viable tumor", "Cell identity", "Enough material", "Pathology first". No patients, surgeons, or identifying information.
Panel 2 heading "2  Compare all four". A perfectly clear 2x2 array of culture wells: top-left "Vehicle", top-right "Drug A", bottom-left "Drug B", bottom-right "A + B". Each contains generic equal starting cell samples and small relevant drug icons. Below exact label "Replicates and dose ranges". Do not make qualitative cell-death outcomes in these wells look like measured patient results.
Panel 3 heading "3  Follow the response". Timeline left to right with an assay meter, a dying-cell icon, then surviving cells regrowing after washout. Labels "Viability", "Cell death", "Drug removal", "Regrowth?". Separate magnifier underneath with labels "Flux", "PARP trapping", "Mechanism checks". Do not imply one commercial assay automatically includes every measurement.
Panel 4 heading "4  Keep the claims separate". Three visually distinct rounded cards connected by dashed arrows with question marks: "Effect in this model" then "Mechanism supported?" then "Patient benefit proven?". First has culture dish, second has molecules, third has a generic clinical-study chart. Small label "Different evidence needed". No checkmark claiming patient benefit and no medical people.
Footer "Better than one drug does not automatically mean synergy".
Accuracy: require both single-agent controls, vehicle and combination. Distinguish reduced metabolism from irreversible death. Ex vivo sensitivity and synergy do not prove patient benefit, particularly recurrence prevention.
