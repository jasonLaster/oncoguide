An educational cartoon explaining that blood testing and breast MRI observe different evidence.

Two large side-by-side panels:
1. “Blood test”: a sample tube contains many neutral DNA fragments and a few distinct tumor-derived fragments. A magnifier examines the tube. Callouts: “Sampled tumor signal” and “Does not locate disease”.
2. “Breast MRI”: a scanner screen shows a schematic breast region with a highlighted imaging finding. A boundary marks the examined region. Callouts: “Features in scanned region” and “Not a cancer-cell count”.

Below both panels, a shared box reads “Combine with clinical and tissue evidence”.
A final small callout reads “No signal ≠ zero disease”.

Flat vector illustration, rounded shapes, friendly cartoon proportions, clean sans-serif labels, matching the composition style of tcr-tcr-t.png. No generic people.

Accuracy guardrails: DNA fragments are outside cells, not intact cancer cells. Blood is a finite sample and cannot identify lesion location. MRI findings reflect imaging features, not direct counts of living cancer cells. Neither panel is a whole-body census. Do not label a negative blood test as cure or show MRI enhancement as proof of viable tumor.

No photorealism, medical photography, logos, watermarks, anatomical horror, extra labels, numerical detection thresholds or treatment recommendations.

Keep semantic colors consistent within each variant: tumor-derived DNA uses one distinct color in the tube, magnifier, legend and Sampled tumor signal callout. Non-tumor DNA uses a different neutral color. Connect the Sampled tumor signal callout only to matching tumor-derived fragments. Never put a non-tumor-colored DNA icon beside that callout.
